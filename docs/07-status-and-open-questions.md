# Status and open questions

> **Status: draft proposal.** This is a draft proposal for discussion and review by the DTGWG (Trust over IP Decentralized Trust Graph Working Group). It is not an adopted or endorsed standard or test suite.


This page lists what is settled for this effort, what is proposed or assumed, and what remains open. "Settled" means agreed within this effort, not accepted by the Trust Tasks task force or by any specification body. Nothing here has been proposed to an outside party.

## 4. Other open items

- O10 is settled and built (the `verifyReceipt` adapter method, group RCPT, docs/04 section 11.6); the rationale, the specification gaps found and the open questions are in [21-vector-group-rationale.md](21-vector-group-rationale.md#7-ceremony-receipts-rcpt). The method is a decision of this effort, not of the task force.
- Witness-share version drift: the keyring wallet imports `witness/session` and `submit` 0.1, the profile targets 0.2 (document 10).
- Witness-share points the keyring wallet does not determine: see document 10 sections 4 and 12 (including the terminal-to-VWC binding gap, reserved as WSH-022, and whether a second, different share on one exchange must be refused, asserted in the permissive reading by the CLARIFY vector WSH-024).
- Build state and what is not built: see [11-build-state.md](11-build-state.md). The IUT-initiated direction of `propose`, `issue` and witness-share (the suite only sends them), IDK, the content of a status task's response and the `continuation` of a status-task hold (not asserted), the initiating side of ceremonies, the client side of the HTTPS binding and the discoverer side of discovery (docs/04 section 11), and the interop runner, recorder and promoter are not built. The out-of-process adapter client, the wire contract (docs/04 section 4), an example server and adapters for VTI, keyring-wallet and its witness server (`src/adapters/`) exist, as do the conformance-implied pair matrix, the ledger view and the artifact-crossed replay ([14-interoperability-tracking.md](14-interoperability-tracking.md)); another implementation writes its own adapter.
- Binding points to confirm (docs/04 section 11.3): whether a request with an unrecognised or missing bearer token is processed as having no sender or refused with 401 (the binding states both; BND-003 covers the missing header and an adapter that refuses declares `binding.requireBearer`; the status table is the Tier 2 `attested` vector BND-007, BND-004 keeping the Content-Type MUST and the 200 case), and whether a recipient mismatch is `identityMismatch` (binding section 3) or `wrongRecipient` (framework 7.2 item 5).
- Ceremony-document points (docs/04 section 11.4): whether a repeated approval must succeed (CER-004, a SHOULD), whether a non-administrator and a missing ceremony must be indistinguishable (CER-007, a MAY), the closed `details.reason` vocabulary for a refused effect and for a reset after the commit (CER-010, CER-012), whether `indeterminate` is the code for an unknown effect outcome (CER-011), and verbatim retention as a profile rule (CER-013) are CLARIFY and never gate. The vectors that pin the suite's own ceremony and two-phase design (CER-003, 004, 006 to 014, 016; 2PC-001 to 005, 008; TIME-103) are channel `proposed`, profile `ceremony-design`, advisory ([decision 39](decisions/04-ceremonies-governance-and-exceptions.md#d39)); CER-003, 008, 009, 014, 2PC-001, 004, 005 and TIME-103 are also Tier 2 `profile-ceremony`. The quorum rule is not asserted by the suite: the ceremony runs relative to the essential governance the adapter declares (docs/04 section 11.4); the native truststackers rule is every named administrator. Whether a begin counts as the proposer's approval is a declared property of the adapter (`ceremony.proposerApproves`, CER-014), not a rule the suite assumes; the status task's response (CER-016) is CLARIFY.
- Task-consent and credential-exchange points (docs/04 section 11.5): the 3b-DTG groups assert a task specification's MUSTs and otherwise stay CLARIFY or Tier 2. Open: whether a decision relayed through the requester (which `task-consent/decision` calls the normal case) passes the framework's issuer and transport-sender check (7.2 items 6 and 7), which refuses a document delivered by someone other than its issuer unless the task states otherwise; whether a decision short of the quorum is answered with the specification's `pending` status or with `trust-task-next-step` (TC-018); which of `expired` and status `refused` is the device's refusal of a lapsed request (TC-015); the order of the decision's refusals (the specification lists `noPending` first, so a non-approver and an unknown request are distinguishable, TC-006); the member name of the nonce in a Data Integrity presentation proof (`nonce` in the text, `challenge` in Data Integrity) and where the nonce comes from (CEX-011 to CEX-014); whether a credential delivered by a relayer rather than its issuer is accepted (the issue specification leaves it to the consumer, so no vector asserts it).
- Discovery points: sorting of `supportedTypes` is a SHOULD (DIS-014, CLARIFY). The abort reason vocabulary and the use of `taskFailed` for an aborted transaction (2PC-002) and the fail-closed answer for an unreadable duplicate record (2PC-008, DUP-007) are design rules of docs/04 section 12, CLARIFY.
- The source revision of the imported task-force fixtures is not recorded (the snapshot had no version-control metadata); re-importing from a checkout fixes this. The licence of the imported fixtures is assumed to follow the code licence.
- Who contacts the task force, and through which channel, is plain open (O14). No contact has been made.
- Remaining engineering work on truststackers (phase references in document 06):
  - **Production ceremony host.** The ceremony-document handlers run against a fixture effect. The utility server's real ceremony coordinator participant does not use them; hooking them to it (name check, governance guard, community creation, fan-out) is a utility-server change plus a core release.
  - **Held-proposal UI and the initiator side.** The agent application has no screen for a held proposal, and the initiating direction of `propose`, `issue` and witness-share is not built.
  - **Legacy TSP participant.** The legacy two-phase-commit path of the VRC receive participant still stores at prepare, so an aborted transaction can leave the credential stored. The boundary path records pending operations at prepare and applies them at commit; the legacy path is untouched.
  - **Gate G2 process wiring and key custody.** The dedicated-key signer, the verifier key policy and the time-pinned resolvers exist at library level but are wired into neither the agent nor the utility server, and nothing stores the dedicated key encrypted. The gate must stay off in any process that signs but cannot switch.
  - **Rotation-log route.** A likely bug (unverified; needs a test before the cutover relies on it): the route that accepts pushed rotation-log entries stores the insertion second as the entry's creation time, while resolution recomputes the entry digest from that column, so an entry whose signed creation time differs would fail chain validation.
  - **Genesis-hash check.** The latest-only resolvers do not check that the identifier is the hash of the genesis entry; decided to add it to `resolveFromLog` (own tested commit, release note); code change pending.
  - **Persistence items K3 and K4 (P11).** K3: the distributed-database log store drops the post-quantum signature of an entry. K4: one document lives in three places (boot document, cached document, log head) and the document-promotion path is unread. Also open from P11: the operations runbook for the cutover, the builder option at provisioning, and conformance of a metadata-only entry with the `did:scid:vh` draft.
  - **Pending code changes already decided:** no recording of read-only evaluate documents; held proposals lapse after 7 days with `deadlineLapsed`.
- Predicted pass/fail results for the two model implementations come from code reading only; nothing was executed against them.
- Truststackers: conformance of a metadata-only log entry with the `did:scid:vh` draft specification (the method is implemented in-repo; the draft was not checked); behaviour of the document-promotion path; whether the utility server's graph route mirrors the agent's canonicalisation; the existing test assertions in the files that phase P5c touches; the accepted-id claim on a real distributed database; whether a legacy install path publishes a bare document with no `authentication` (document 06 section 6).
- Key-rotation vectors are outside the first benchmark; their design is unverified (document 04 section 10).
